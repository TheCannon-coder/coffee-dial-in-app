# Chris's weekly playbook

The agents do the reading, drafting, and shipping. These are the five things only
you do, written for someone doing them for the first time. Time budget: 1–2 hours
a week, spread out. Nothing here is urgent on the day; everything keeps.

---

## The rhythm

| When | What | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Read the memo, make the decisions in "Waiting on Chris" | 20–30 min |
| Mon–Fri mornings | Post one of the week's five | 5 min each |
| When a batch lands (every ~2 weeks) | Send the creator notes you like | 15–20 min |
| When asked (rare) | Approve a new email template from your phone | 5 min |
| Any time | Give feedback on anything that felt off | 1 sentence |

Where things live: everything is in the GitHub repo under `marketing/` —
`memos/` (Monday), `social/<date>/` (posts), `outreach/<date>.md` (creator
notes), `content/` (site changes and the experiments board), `LOG.md` (the
running record). Agent runs are listed at https://claude.ai/code/routines.

---

## 1. Monday: the memo (20–30 min)

**What it is.** One page, written at 7am by the Scorecard agent, from the week's
data. It never asks you to do work — it asks you to decide things.

**How to read it, in order:**
1. The headline line: weekly active brewers vs. the target (20 by Jan 15). Just
   note the number; a change of one or two is noise and the memo will say so.
2. *What moved* — the agent's best read of why. You're allowed to disagree; you
   know things it doesn't (a post went viral, a machine broke, a friend signed up).
3. *This week's one recommendation* — the single thing it thinks is worth doing.
   Your job is yes / no / "not yet, because…". You don't have to explain yourself,
   but when you do, the reason gets written into the rules and the agents get smarter.
4. *Waiting on Chris* — the actual to-do list. Usually two or three items.
5. *Data problems* — skim it. If it says a source is stale, that's for me to fix,
   not you.

**What good looks like.** You close the session with every "Waiting on Chris"
item answered. That's it. I log the decisions, update the rules, and the agents
act on them during the week.

**What to do when unsure.** Say "I don't know" — it's a valid decision. The
default for anything with money or your name on it is "hold." The default for
site changes is "let it run."

## 2. Posting (5 min per post, one a day)

**What it is.** Each week the Social agent leaves five posts in
`marketing/social/<date>/posts.md` with cards as PNGs. You post them. Nothing
posts without you.

**Getting them onto your phone (once a week, 3 minutes on the Mac):**
1. In our Monday session I send you the five captions and the card PNGs as files.
2. Open **Notes** on the Mac. Make a note called *CBC posts* and paste the five
   captions in. Notes syncs to your phone on its own.
3. AirDrop the PNGs to your phone, or drop them in **Photos** (iCloud syncs them).

**Posting one (on the phone):**
1. Instagram → **+** → **Post** → pick the card (or the screenshot the post names).
2. **Next** → **Next** (skip filters).
3. Caption: open Notes, copy that post's caption, paste. Hashtags stay in the
   caption; they're already there.
4. **Advanced settings → Write alt text** → paste the alt text from the same
   post. (Takes 20 seconds; it's how blind users and search engines read the image.)
5. **Share.**

**When.** One a day, mornings — 7 to 9am Toronto is when home brewers are
holding a cup. Monday through Friday. If you miss a day, post two the next; if
you miss the week, they're not perishable.

**After posting (the part that matters more than the post).** Check back once
that evening. Reply to every comment in your own words — a sentence is plenty.
A question gets a real answer. Nobody gets sold to in a comment. If someone
says their coffee is bitter, tell them the one thing, like you would at the bar.

**What good looks like.** Five posts, five mornings, every comment answered
within a day. Likes don't matter yet; saves and comments do. The Scorecard
doesn't see Instagram, so tell me on Monday if anything got a real reaction.

**What to skip.** Stories, reels, and TikTok are a later lesson. Don't make
extra posts yourself unless you want to — the five are the job.

## 3. Creator notes (15–20 min when a batch lands)

**What it is.** Every two weeks the Outreach agent leaves five creators and a
draft note to each in `marketing/outreach/<date>.md`. The agent could only
*search* for them, not open their pages, so each one carries a **Verify before
sending** line.

**For each creator:**
1. Open their profile on your phone. Spend two minutes: are they real, active,
   and is the piece of content the note quotes actually theirs? If not, skip —
   never send a note with a wrong detail.
2. Check how to reach them: DMs open? A "contact" or business email on their
   profile? For a Substack writer, subscribing (free) and **replying to their
   next newsletter email** reaches them directly — that's often the best path.
3. Fix the first name if the draft guessed or left it blank.
4. Paste and send. Don't polish; the draft is already in your voice.
5. Tell me "sent 1, 3, 5" on Monday and I'll mark the table. (Or edit the file
   on GitHub yourself — pencil icon — when you're comfortable.)

**What to expect.** One reply in five is normal. Replies are usually warm.
If someone joins, they'll appear in the affiliate dashboard and the Scorecard
will mention it. Never follow up more than once, and never re-pitch someone who
said no.

**What good looks like.** Three to five sent per batch, each to a profile you
actually looked at.

## 4. Approving an email template (5 min, rarely)

New lifecycle emails (the post-first-brew check-in, the "was the fix better?"
note, the Pro offer) need your yes once each. I'll send a `[TEST]` to your inbox.
Read it on your phone like a customer would. Reply with "good," or "change X."
After that it sends on its own.

---

## 5. Feedback (the most important job)

The agents don't learn from results on their own; they learn from you. When
something feels off — too cheeky, wrong fact, wrong priority, not how you'd say
it — tell me *what* and, if you can, *why*. The fix is one line in that agent's
instruction file, and it's different on the next run. Examples that already
happened: "percentages, not raw counts" and "The Cannon is a credential, not a
channel" — both are rules now.

Also tell me when something is *right*. That gets written down too, so the next
draft doesn't drift away from what worked.

---

## What to ignore

- Push notifications from agent runs. They're the agent talking to itself.
- GitHub emails about commits. The log has everything that matters.
- A run that "failed" or produced nothing. Usually it's being careful (it won't
  invent a creator or email a stale list). I check these.

## Two-week on-ramp

- **Week 1:** post one a day; send one creator note; read Monday's memo with me
  on the call and decide together.
- **Week 2:** the full rhythm on your own; I'm there Monday to debrief.
- **After:** Monday is yours, 20–30 minutes; everything else is five-minute
  habits.
